What happens at 10 requirements?

The test uses five must-haves. Real jobs will have more. This page stress-tests the three displays at up to 10 requirements and shows one way recruiters could configure them. It is separate from the test links, so participants are unaffected.

A · % fit

One failure disappears

The percentage is an average. With 5 requirements a failed must-have costs about 20 points; with 10 it costs about 10, so a candidate who fails a deal-breaker still reads as "Excellent fit". The arithmetic tooltip also grows from five terms to ten, which makes it harder to read, not easier.

If we keep a score: cap it when any must-have fails, and never let the band hide a failed must-have.

B · Band and count

"8 of 10" hides which two

A count treats every requirement as equal. "8 of 10 met" might be two nice-to-haves missing or two deal-breakers failed, and the recruiter cannot tell without clicking. Band thresholds also need a rule that scales (is 8 of 10 the same as 4 of 5?).

If we keep a count: count must-haves only, and add "1 not met" next to it.

C · Criteria and evidence

The row stops being scannable

Ten chips wrap onto two or three lines, row height goes from about 50px to 130px in this demo (13 candidates per screen drops to 5), and the chip labels start to collide ("Accountant role" vs "Audit support"). Recruiters then compare rows by reading, which is what the chips were meant to avoid. The drawer also becomes a ten-block scroll.

If we keep chips: show problems first (not met, unknown) and collapse the met ones into one count; group the drawer by status.
